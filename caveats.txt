CAVEATS  (kept up to date by Claude; last updated 2026-10-30)
=====================================================================
DATA
 1. No 2026 data exists. 2026 features are the 2024 CES (and 2024 ACS) carried
    forward. State demographics and partisanship will have drifted.
 2. The regression targets are CES pre-election Senate INTENT, not votes. This is
    the only source with an "undecided" answer. Intent over-represents people
    who answer surveys, and self-reported vote skews toward winners.
 3. "Undecided" = Not Sure + No One + third-party/other. It is not "will not vote";
    turnout is not modelled. Predicted shares are shares of survey respondents,
    not of ballots cast.
 4. Certified results (MIT Election Lab, 2016-2024) only check the Dem share of the TWO-PARTY
    vote; they cannot validate the Undecided share.
 5. Small states have few respondents (WY ~95 in CES 2024), so their features and
    targets are noisy.
 6. "Republican %" / "Democrat %" features come from the same respondents as the
    targets: partly circular. ACS features (no party info) are the clean check.
 7. 2014-2020 CES rows have intent_sen (checked on download). Senate answers per year:
    2014 27,313; 2016 46,459; 2018 42,848; 2020 26,000; so 2014 and 2020 states are noisier.
 7b. Cycle-to-cycle swings are large: predicting 2018 from the other cycles has 8.1 pp
    error (vs 5-6 pp for other cycles), so a single-cycle model is a poor guide to a new year.

MODEL
 8. Only ~34 state-races per cycle. Ridge regression with LOO-chosen penalty is used
    to limit overfitting; with 15 features on 34 rows (2022-only or 2024-only models)
    the fit is fragile. More cycles (midterms / all) should be steadier.
 9. Midterm effect is a single 0/1 flag, learned only from midterm cycles in the
    training set. The 2024-only model cannot learn it (flag is constant).
10. No candidate-level information (incumbency, quality, spending, scandals, specials).
    Each state is treated as a generic Democrat-vs-Republican contest.
11. Independents who caucus with a party are coded by it (King, Sanders -> Democrat). Osborn
    (NE 2024) does not caucus, so stays 'other'; other independents/third parties (Orman KS 2014,
    Pressler SD 2014, Stock AK 2016, McMullin UT 2022) also stay 'other'. 'Caucuses with' is a
    judgement about future affiliation, not a ballot label.
12. Special and regular races are treated alike: a race counts if it has both a Democrat
    (after coding) and a Republican, and a state with two such races in one year pools them
    (GA 2020, OK, SC, MN, MS, CA 2022). CES records one Senate answer per respondent, so a
    pooled state-year can differ slightly from what a respondent had in mind. State-years with
    no Dem-v-Rep contest (CA 2016/2018, NE 2024 regular) are dropped.
13. Predictions are point estimates; no uncertainty intervals yet.

LEAKAGE AND BASELINES
17. Residual: CES case_ids do not repeat across cycles in the cumulative file, so panel
    overlap between survey waves cannot be checked. Panel respondents would make lagged
    features correlated with later targets (not future information, but dependence).
18. Forward-chaining test sets are small (about 30 races per cycle); winner accuracy
    differences of 1-3 points (a few races) are within noise.
19. 'last Senate result' is undefined for states with no earlier Senate race in the
    data; baselines are compared only on rows where every method has a prediction.
20. Leakage from same-year features was modest and noisy (about 0.5 pp for the survey-only
    'all' set; the opposite sign in some sets), so it is a risk control, not the main driver.

PREVIOUS-RESULT FEATURES
21. The headline model is chosen on 3 validation cycles (about 100 races, strongly correlated
    within a cycle) and scored on one holdout cycle (33 races); differences of about a point
    between candidates are within noise. On the 2024 holdout, 'prior' alone happened to beat
    the chosen model (2.8 vs 5.6 pp); that is the expected cost of selecting on validation.
22. Where a state had no earlier Senate race the Senate feature falls back to the
    presidential share (flagged). Previous presidential results are 2-4 years old,
    depending on whether the target is a midterm or presidential year.
23. A linear ridge on the three-way log-ratio is not guaranteed to match a raw baseline
    with the same information: the 'last presidential result' baseline has lower error
    (4.0 pp) than ridge on previous results (5.0 pp).

MIDTERM-ONLY MODELS AND HEADLINE
24. Midterm-only models train on 1 cycle (to predict 2018) or 2 cycles (to predict 2022) and 3
    cycles for 2026, so they have far less data than all-cycle models; the midterm flag is
    constant in them. Their worse accuracy partly reflects sample size, not just midterm-vs-
    presidential differences.
25. The headline feature set (ces+acs) beat ces+prior on validation by 0.05 pp: a tie. The
    choice among feature sets is not statistically meaningful; averaging models would be safer.

SIMULATED DATA
14. Simulated results (data/00-simulated_data/) come from a known generating
    process. High accuracy there shows the code works; it says nothing about
    real 2026 accuracy. Do not quote simulated numbers as findings.

SITE
16. The Quarto page and figures show REAL-data results (SOURCE = "real"); simulated PNGs
    (paper/figures/simulated_*) are kept only to demonstrate the pipeline.
    The tile map saturates colours beyond 20%/80% Dem two-party share.

ENVIRONMENT
15. ACS was downloaded successfully (variable codes confirmed). Only 12 variables are
    used; ACS adds no accuracy on real data, likely because CES already captures
    demographics and party ID, and there are only ~34 races per cycle. The ACS variable codes
    (B01001, B01002, B02001, B03003, B15003, B19013) were written from knowledge of
    the Census API and need a first real run to confirm.
