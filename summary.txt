PROJECT SUMMARY  (kept up to date by Claude; last updated 2026-10-30 (previous results as features))
=====================================================================
Goal: forecast every 2026 US Senate race (35 contests) with a state-level
regression, as a transparent baseline next to the LLM "silicon sampling" forecast.
Senate seats are decided by statewide POPULAR vote (no Electoral College), so
vote share per state is the right target.

Method (scripts/05-regression.py)
 - Unit of analysis: one row per state per election cycle.
 - Features (LAGGED two years: cycle y uses the survey/ACS of y-2): weighted CES state profile (male, white, Black, Hispanic, college,
   mean age, % Republican, % Democrat) + ACS demographics (optional) + midterm flag.
 - Targets: three shares summing to 1: Democratic, Republican, Undecided/other.
 - Model: ridge regression on additive log-ratios (multi-class, stays on the
   simplex), penalty by leave-one-out CV.
 - Three training sets for the 2026 forecast: 2022 only, 2024 only, 2022+2024.
   Every single cycle (2014 ... 2024) is also a training set, plus 2014+2016+2018,
   "midterms" (2014, 2018, 2022) and "all" (six cycles) when the data are present.
 - Tests: forward-chaining (train on earlier cycles, predict the next; the honest
   forecast test) and leave-one-state-out (optimistic: sees same-cycle states); MAE
   of each share vs. a fold-wise mean-share baseline; winner accuracy; error vs.
   certified two-party share. Same-year-feature tables are kept only to show the leak.
 - 2026 features carry forward the 2024 CES (and ACS).

Status
 - Every one of the 35 2026 races has D/R/Undecided percentages (sum to 100%) under every
   training set, on simulated and on real 2022/2024 CES data.

 - Pipeline runs end to end on simulated data (--source simulated) and on the real
   2022/2024 CES.
 - Real-data results so far (CES features only; targets are CES intent): Dem-share
   MAE about 4.6-5.7 pp vs 7-10 pp for a mean baseline; winner accuracy 82-94%.
   
 - CES 2014-2024 now downloaded; the real regression runs on all five cycles (CES
   features only). Real-data leave-one-cycle-out error for the 'midterms' set is
   WORSE than the baseline (10.2 vs 9.6 pp Dem MAE); 'all' and '2022+2024' beat it.
 - LEAKAGE AUDIT (details in paper/regression.qmd): same-year features, ACS vintage,
   same-cycle information in leave-one-state-out, time-travel schemes, a baseline that
   saw the held-out state, and a 2026 forecast trained on a different feature timing.
   All fixed or labelled; certified results are never used to fit a model.
 - Honest real-data accuracy (lagged features, forward-chaining, 'all' training set):
   Dem two-party MAE 5.9 pp, winners correct 88%. Leaky same-year features gave 5.2 pp.
 - SIMPLE BASELINES on identical state-races (139 races, 2016-2024, vs certified results):
     last Senate result 89.9% winners / 5.5 pp,  last presidential result 89.2% / 4.3 pp,
     party ID + leaners 87.8% / 5.1 pp,  ridge (ces) 87.1% / 5.6 pp,  party ID (pid3)
     80.6% / 6.2 pp,  majority class and mean share 52.5%.
   => Survey-only regression does NOT beat the best simple baselines at calling winners.
 - PREVIOUS RESULTS AS FEATURES (feature sets 'prior' and 'ces+prior': last presidential
   and last Senate Dem two-party share, strictly earlier years; Senate share falls back to
   the presidential one + a flag where a state had no earlier Senate race):
   forward-chaining, 'all' training set, lagged: ces 5.9 pp / 88% winners, ces+acs 5.8 / 88%,
   prior 5.0 / 90%, ces+prior 5.0 / 90%. On the 139 common races: prior 89.9% winners
   (ties the best baseline, last Senate result) and 5.1 pp; ces+prior 89.2% / 4.9 pp;
   last presidential result alone still has the lowest error (4.3 pp).
   => Previous results lift the regression to the level of the best baselines but not
      clearly past them; the survey adds little once previous results are known.
 - ACS downloaded. Adding ACS features does not improve accuracy on real data (about
   equal or slightly worse than CES-only); CES-only is the recommended primary set.
 - Real-data figures: paper/figures/real_*.png. The Quarto page now runs on real data.
