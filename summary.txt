PROJECT SUMMARY  (kept up to date by Claude; last updated 2026-10-30 (coding decisions, midterm-only models, all-cycle headline))
=====================================================================
Goal: forecast every 2026 US Senate race (35 contests) with a state-level
regression, as a transparent baseline next to the LLM "silicon sampling" forecast.
Senate seats are decided by statewide POPULAR vote (no Electoral College), so
vote share per state is the right target.

Method (scripts/05-regression.py)
 - Unit of analysis: one row per state per election cycle.
 - Features (LAGGED two years: cycle y uses the survey/ACS of y-2): weighted CES state profile (male, white, Black, Hispanic, college,
   mean age, % Republican, % Democrat) + ACS demographics (optional) + midterm flag.
 - Targets: three shares summing to 1: Democratic, Republican, Undecided/other.
 - Model: ridge regression on additive log-ratios (multi-class, stays on the
   simplex), penalty by leave-one-out CV.
 - Three training sets for the 2026 forecast: 2022 only, 2024 only, 2022+2024.
   Every single cycle (2014 ... 2024) is also a training set, plus 2014+2016+2018,
   "midterms" (2014, 2018, 2022) and "all" (six cycles) when the data are present.
 - Tests: forward-chaining (train on earlier cycles, predict the next; the honest
   forecast test) and leave-one-state-out (optimistic: sees same-cycle states); MAE
   of each share vs. a fold-wise mean-share baseline; winner accuracy; error vs.
   certified two-party share. Same-year-feature tables are kept only to show the leak.
 - 2026 features carry forward the 2024 CES (and ACS).

Status (real data, CES 2012-2024, ACS, certified Senate + presidential results)
 - Coding decisions: independents who caucus with a party are coded by it (King ME, Sanders VT ->
   Democrat; Osborn NE does not caucus, stays "other"); special and regular races are treated
   alike (state-year races with a D and an R candidate are pooled). The CES Senate question
   already puts King and Sanders in its Democratic slot. This fixed Maine 2018/2024 (previously
   scored with a near-zero Democratic share) and added Vermont 2018/2024: 191 state-races.
 - HEADLINE: trains on ALL SIX cycles (2014-2024), by decision. The feature set is chosen by
   validation (scripts/05 select_model): validation cycles 2018/2020/2022, holdout 2024 never used
   to choose. Chosen = ces+acs (validation 4.92 pp, 88% winners); on the 2024 holdout 5.6 pp, 88%
   winners. ces+prior was a near-tie (4.97 pp); on the holdout 'prior' alone was best (2.8 pp).
 - LEAKAGE AUDIT (paper/regression.qmd): same-year features, ACS vintage, same-cycle information
   in leave-one-state-out, time-travel schemes, a baseline that saw the held-out state, and a
   forecast trained on a different feature timing. Fixed or labelled; certified results never fit
   a model. Same-year features flattered error by about 0.5 pp (5.1 vs 5.6 pp, 'ces', all cycles).
 - Honest accuracy (lagged features, forward-chaining, all six cycles, Dem two-party vs results):
   ces 5.6 pp / 89% winners, ces+acs 5.5 / 89%, prior 5.0 / 89%, ces+prior 4.9 / 89%.
 - SIMPLE BASELINES on identical races (141, 2016-2024): last presidential result 90.8% winners /
   4.0 pp; party ID + leaners 89.4% / 4.7; ridge prior 89.4% / 5.0; ridge ces & ces+acs 88.7% / 5.3;
   ridge ces+prior 88.7% / 4.8; last Senate result 87.9% / 5.6; party ID (pid3) 82.3% / 5.7;
   majority class and mean share 50.4%. The regression does not clearly beat the best baselines;
   its distinct value is the three-way share forecast including Undecided.
 - MIDTERM-ONLY MODELS (fit on 2014, 2018, 2022; predict 2026): worse than all-cycle models on the
   same later midterms (pooled ces+acs 6.3 vs 5.1 pp; ces 6.1 vs 4.9), because they train on only
   one or two cycles. For 2026 they flip no winners relative to the headline (35 races compared).
 - 2026 forecast (headline): 14 Democratic, 21 Republican wins; every race has D/R/Undecided
   percentages summing to 100% under every feature set and training set.
